# Hip shell: decisions and assumptions register (Idle T-czv2d8)

Every decision and assumption the hip-shell work rests on, with who made it, why, its visual explainer, and what
changes if it is reversed. The owner reviews this list. Nothing marked **proposed** or **assumed** is final until the
owner says so. When an entry changes, it is edited in place (git keeps the history), and every artefact that
depends on it is listed under *Depends on it*.

Status values:
- **owner**: decided by the owner.
- **assumed**: the agent's working assumption, open for review.
- **proposed**: the agent recommends it and waits for the owner.
- **reversed**: no longer valid.

## Hip actuator pattern (owner question 2026-11-16: why G1, GR-2, Walker S2 and Digit hips look different)

| ID | Entry | Status | Explainer |
|---|---|---|---|
| A-16 | **Where the difference comes from (study).** Measured on our CAD; reference robots from the S2 table.<br>(1) **Attachment.** In every reference the pelvis holds the first hip actuator along that actuator's own axis: a pitch disc on G1, GR-2, H2 and Booster, a roll disc on Walker S2 and Digit. The leg meets the torso through one rotation about that axis, so the seam is a circle that never moves. Our leg hangs from a vertical yaw servo in the torso through a 51 mm neck: the torso enters the hip radially from above, and yaw swings the pitch servo (45 mm inboard), the pitch bracket and the leg under the pelvis. 5 of the 6 worst realistic-pose contacts are yaw-driven, e.g. the toes-in climb step: 9,200 mm³ against the battery cradle.<br>(2) **Actuator shape.** Pancake actuators are discs centred on their axis. Our bus servos are bricks with the shaft 10 mm from one end: off-centre by 9–14 mm, reaching r 31–37 about their own axis.<br>(3) **Brackets.** U-brackets (horn + idler) straddle the parent servo and reach r 48 (pitch) and r 67 (roll).<br>(4) **Axis order.** Yaw-first puts a radial torso neck into the side view, which makes the globe skin need nested bands (A-08).
